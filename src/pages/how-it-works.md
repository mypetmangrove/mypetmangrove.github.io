    heading: 1. Collect
    subheading: Start with a seed, spark a movement
    lead: Students and volunteers become citizen scientists, gathering native propagules that wash ashore.
    body: |-
      Every tide washes mangrove propagules ashore. Thousands of them. Most dry out and die.

      - **Free.** The trees are already on the beach.
      - **Local.** Collected from the coastline we restore, so they're adapted to it.
      - **Real fieldwork.** Students log where, when, and in what condition they found each one.
    image: /images/site/step-collect.jpg

  - type: split
    anchor: grow
    background: mist
    image_side: left
    heading: 2. Grow
    subheading: Your class pet, our planet's future
    lead: Each seedling becomes a living lesson, growing alongside its caretaker.
    body: |-
      - **A class pet.** Students check the water, watch new roots and leaves, and give them names.
      - **Patience and responsibility**, learned by doing, not by worksheet.
      - **Grow-out time:** 6 to 15 weeks in a jar of water.

      Teachers track every seedling, check-in, and photo in the [teacher portal](/teacher-portal/).
    image: /images/site/step-grow.jpg
    image_alt: A mangrove seedling with its first leaves

    heading: 3. Learn
    subheading: Learn the science. Lead the change.
    lead: Hands-on experiments and a state-aligned curriculum show how mangroves really work.
    body: |-
      - **Blue carbon:** how mangroves store carbon.
      - **Coastal resilience:** how roots protect shorelines.
      - **Marine life:** how the forest feeds and shelters fish.

      Example lesson: Carbon (Balloon) Capture.

      - **The loop restarts.** Next year's class collects new propagules.
